10.2 Experiment design

As v1.4.1 does not introduce new parameters explored in relation to v1.4, the same Sobol-sampled values are used here. Notice however that this model does introduce new constants used to determine the correspondence between age and the adult-equivalent unit (i.e., 15, 45, and the exponents 1.2 and 2; see get-carrying-capacity-demand-at-age).

As one of the daily iteration versions, only 512 configurations of the original Sobol exploration sample ((n=2,000)) was used to obtain sufficiently-robust results. The end states data set then contains 12,288 runs (512 configurations × 2 residence rules × 4 Coale-Demeny regions × 3 replicates).

A particularity in processing the data with age-weighted variables is that we need to use (or compute) the total carrying capacity demand (carrying_capacity_demand) and use it to calculate pressure instead of using totalIndividuals directly (i.e., pressure = carrying_capacity_demand / carrying_capacity). Although this variable can be exported through NetLogo’s Behavior Space, it was not specified for all massive Sobol sampled experiments, so it is necessary to calculate it using womenAgeStructure and menAgeStructure (see “library/density/compute_carrying_capacity_demand.R”).

Because of the sheer number of daily iterations required to approach comparable time scales of previous versions, trajectories datasets are simply too large to be loaded conventionally in R. For this reason, we need to use the function argument ignored_variables (alternatively also selected_variables is available) to skip loading unnecessary variables, as well as potentially restricting the span of the simulation data to a subset of iteration steps (start_step, end_step).

The distribution of end states is broadly similar across Coale-Demeny regions, with extinction and low-pressure persistence accounting for most outcomes and high-pressure persistence forming a smaller share. Under the selected parameter configuration, both residence systems rapidly approach bounded population levels and remain viable throughout the simulation horizon. Individual trajectories fluctuate around their long-run range, but neither residence system exhibits sustained expansion or systematic collapse.

Population levels are also remarkably similar between residence rules. Age-weighted density regulation therefore produces a robust high-pressure equilibrium under this favourable demographic configuration, with residence organisation contributing comparatively little to the realised abundance.

Because pressure is based on age-weighted carrying-capacity demand rather than headcount, the population can remain numerically above the value of carrying_capacity without necessarily representing an equivalent ecological overshoot. The regulatory state must therefore be interpreted through demand-weighted pressure rather than population size alone.

Population-pressure regime is strongly associated with demographic composition. Residence organisation produces a consistent but very small direct difference in age structure. Matrilocal-matrilineal populations are slightly older and contain somewhat smaller juvenile shares across all Coale-Demeny regions, but the aggregate effect is only (d=0.16). Regional mortality families likewise produce comparatively modest variation.

Pressure regime, rather than residence rule or mortality-region category, is consequently the principal structural distinction among surviving populations.

Population pressure is controlled jointly by demographic recruitment and ecological feedback. Fertility peak intensity is the strongest predictor, with higher fertility increasing age-weighted demand relative to carrying capacity. density_effect_scaling_factor follows closely and has the opposite effect: stronger density-dependent mortality lowers the pressure maintained by surviving populations.

10.5.4.15 Interpretation

Fertility peak intensity remains the principal determinant of age structure in v1.4.1. Fertility spread, male union timing and pre-peak union spread, and union peak intensities provide secondary influences, while postpartum amenorrhoea contributes a smaller but consistent ageing effect through birth spacing.

Baseline mortality has virtually no direct association with median age among surviving populations. The different response variables therefore reveal a useful division of demographic roles: fertility and union formation primarily shape cohort composition, whereas mortality and ecological feedback are more consequential for survival and population pressure.

Demand weighting adds a direct connection between these demographic structures and ecological load, because the age composition generated by reproductive processes now determines the total carrying-capacity demand exerted by the population.

10.6 Discussion notes

Version 1.4.1 combines daily demographic scheduling with age-weighted ecological demand. Population pressure is therefore determined not simply by the number of individuals but by the demand generated by their age composition, while births, deaths, union formation, gestation, and postpartum amenorrhoea unfold through the daily demographic cycle.

The model produces a broad range of end states, dominated by extinction and low-pressure persistence, with high-pressure persistence less frequent and overshooting rare. Under the favourable reference configuration, however, both residence systems converge on highly stable Stress-prone trajectories. Demand-weighted density dependence can therefore maintain populations close to ecological constraint without producing substantial long-run oscillation.

Fertility peak intensity remains the dominant demographic control. It strongly determines survival, population pressure, and median age. Density-effect strength provides the principal ecological counterweight, reducing realised pressure as feedback intensifies. Baseline mortality contributes to survival and pressure but has little direct effect on age structure among survivors.

Age weighting makes demographic composition ecologically consequential. The same reproductive mechanisms that determine juvenile recruitment and median age also alter total carrying-capacity demand, creating a direct pathway from reproductive schedules to ecological pressure.

Residence rule remains secondary at the aggregate level. Patrilocal-patrilineal populations show a small survival advantage in the global exploration, while matrilocal-matrilineal populations are slightly older on average. In the targeted trajectory experiment, however, both residence systems occupy virtually identical Stress-prone regimes. Residence effects are therefore conditional and are expressed more clearly through particular sex-specific union parameters than through a general difference in resilience.

The comparison with the yearly demand-weighted formulation indicates that temporal resolution changes the quantitative expression of demographic turnover and pressure, but the daily model should not be regarded simply as a smoother version of the yearly one. Explicit reproductive delays and event timing alter the pathway through which demographic demand accumulates, even though the principal fertility-driven architecture remains recognisable.

10.7 Chapter conclusion

Version 1.4.1 establishes the daily demand-weighted density-regulated formulation of the Household Demography model. Fertility peak intensity remains the dominant demographic driver, while density-effect strength provides the principal ecological regulation of population pressure. Age-weighted demand adds an explicit compositional dimension by making ecological load depend on who comprises the population rather than on headcount alone.

Surviving populations are usually maintained below the nominal demand threshold, although favourable parameter combinations can sustain stable high-pressure states for centuries. Age structure remains governed primarily by fertility, union formation, and birth spacing, whereas baseline mortality and density regulation act more strongly on viability and ecological pressure.

The model therefore provides the appropriate daily demand-side foundation for v1.4.2, where productive labour is added to the supply side and effective carrying capacity becomes endogenous to demographic composition.
