11.2 Experiment design

As version v1.4.2 introduces a new parameter, labour_demand_per_capita, an additional batch of Sobol sampled values is generated. As in v1.4.1, this model does require constants used to determine the correspondence between age and the adult-equivalent unit (i.e., 15, 45, and the exponent 10, which determines a steeper decline compared with the function for demand; see get-labour-at-age).

As one of the daily iteration versions, only 512 configurations of the original Sobol exploration sample ((n=2,000)) was used to obtain sufficiently-robust results. The end states data set then contains 12,288 runs (512 configurations × 2 residence rules × 4 Coale-Demeny regions × 3 replicates).

A particularity in processing the data with age-weighted variables is that we need to use (or compute) the total carrying capacity demand (carrying_capacity_demand) and use it to calculate pressure instead of using totalIndividuals directly (i.e., pressure = carrying_capacity_demand / carrying_capacity). Although this variable can be exported through NetLogo’s Behavior Space, it was not specified for all massive Sobol sampled experiments, so it is necessary to calculate it using womenAgeStructure and menAgeStructure (see “library/density/compute_carrying_capacity_demand.R”).

Because of the sheer number of daily iterations required to approach comparable time scales of previous versions, trajectories datasets are simply too large to be loaded conventionally in R. For this reason, we need to use the function argument ignored_variables (alternatively also selected_variables is available) to skip loading unnecessary variables, as well as potentially restricting the span of the simulation data to a subset of iteration steps (start_step, end_step). The different trajectory-type data frames must be loaded, preprocessed, saved as RData, and cleared, so that only one is in memory simultaneously.

The addition of labour-dependent effective carrying capacity imposes a strong viability constraint across the explored parameter space. Extinction is by far the most frequent end state under both residence rules and across all Coale-Demeny regions, while only a comparatively small subset of parameter configurations supports long-term persistence.

Among surviving populations, both low- and high-pressure states occur, while overshooting is uncommon. In v1.4.2, these categories integrate both sides of the demographic-ecological balance: pressure expresses age-weighted carrying-capacity demand relative to the effective capacity that can actually be realised through available labour. A population may therefore experience high pressure either because demographic demand is large, because productive labour constrains effective capacity, or through both processes simultaneously.

Residence rule and Coale-Demeny region produce comparatively modest differences in the aggregate distribution of outcomes. The dominant pattern is the strong restriction of the viable parameter space produced by the interaction between demographic demand and labour-mediated productive capacity.

Under the selected reference configuration, both residence systems remain viable throughout the simulation horizon and rapidly converge towards bounded population levels. Individual stochastic trajectories fluctuate around these long-run levels, but there is no evidence of systematic decline or delayed collapse in either residence system.

The realised population sizes are also strikingly similar between residence rules. Because regulation is based on age-weighted demand relative to labour-mediated effective carrying capacity, the numerical relationship between headcount and nominal carrying_capacity is not itself sufficient to diagnose ecological pressure. The relevant quantity is the demand generated by the population relative to the capacity that its available labour can actually realise.

These trajectories therefore represent a favourable demographic configuration in which productive labour remains sufficient to support stable long-term regulation.

The global end-state distributions show that labour limitation remains active among populations that survive. Labour ratios span values both below and above unity, and substantial overlap occurs among low-pressure persistence, high-pressure persistence, and overshooting outcomes. Survival therefore does not require populations to maintain enough labour to realise the full nominal carrying capacity.

When labour supply is insufficient, effective carrying capacity falls below the nominal environmental maximum. Because population pressure is calculated as age-weighted demand divided by this effective capacity, a reduction in productive capacity directly raises pressure even if demographic demand remains unchanged. Labour limitation can consequently intensify density-dependent mortality without requiring an increase in population abundance.

At the same time, populations can persist while effective capacity remains partially constrained. Labour limitation therefore does not operate simply as a binary sufficiency test: it continuously changes the ecological denominator against which demographic demand is evaluated.

The reference trajectory ensemble represents a relatively labour-sufficient part of this behavioural space. Its labour ratios remain above unity through most of the simulation, allowing laboured_share to remain close to one and effective carrying capacity to approach its nominal environmental maximum. Across the global exploration, however, viable states also occur under sustained partial labour limitation.

11.5.4.15 Interpretation

Age structure in v1.4.2 remains governed primarily by reproductive and union-formation processes rather than by labour demand or mortality intensity. Fertility peak intensity provides the strongest common control, while fertility timing and spread, male union timing and pre-peak union spread, and union peak intensities contribute important secondary effects.

Several fertility and female union parameters now show residence-dependent or even opposing associations, indicating that their marginal structural effects become more conditional once labour-mediated viability strongly filters the surviving population. Male union timing and pre-peak spread remain particularly influential under patrilocal-patrilineal residence.

Labour demand per capita, despite dominating extinction and population pressure, contributes relatively little directly to median age. Its principal role is therefore selective: it determines which demographic configurations remain viable much more strongly than it determines the composition of those populations once they survive.

11.7 Discussion notes

Version 1.4.2 completes the demand-and-supply formulation of density regulation under daily demographic scheduling. Age-weighted subsistence demand defines the demographic burden placed on the system, while age-weighted labour determines how much of the nominal environmental capacity can actually be realised. Population pressure combines these components directly as demand relative to effective carrying capacity.

The addition of productive capacity sharply restricts the viable parameter space. labour_demand_per_capita becomes the strongest predictor of extinction and population pressure, showing that viability depends critically on whether the productive structure of the population can sustain sufficient effective capacity relative to its own demographic demand.

Labour limitation nevertheless need not produce immediate collapse. Surviving populations can persist with effective carrying capacity below the nominal environmental maximum, provided the resulting demand-to-capacity ratio remains compatible with density-dependent mortality. Productive scarcity therefore operates continuously through population pressure as well as acting as a viability constraint.

The sensitivity analyses distinguish this ecological coupling from the mechanisms structuring population composition. Fertility and union formation remain the principal determinants of median age, while labour demand strongly shapes the pressure environment within which those demographic configurations can survive.

Productive limitation does not reduce all surviving populations to a single labour-sufficient state. Across the global exploration, viable populations occur with labour ratios below unity, demonstrating that partial realisation of nominal capacity can form part of a persistent regulated regime. The reference trajectory configuration occupies a more favourable region of the parameter space and maintains labour ratios above unity, illustrating that labour sufficiency and labour-constrained persistence are both possible outcomes of the same mechanism.

Internal demographic composition remains governed largely by a different set of controls. Fertility and union formation dominate median age, while labour demand and baseline mortality have comparatively weak direct effects among survivors. The supply constraint therefore acts more strongly as a filter on demographic viability than as a continuous determinant of age composition.

Residence organisation modifies several of these relationships, most clearly in male union timing and pre-peak union spread and in the association between pressure regime and median age. Yet residence differences remain conditional rather than constituting a general hierarchy of resilience.

The targeted labour-demand sweeps make the interaction explicit. A high labour requirement can strongly reduce effective capacity and trigger high pressure or extinction under one demographic configuration, while remaining compatible with full productive capacity under another. There is therefore no universal labour threshold independent of mortality, fertility, age structure, and residence organisation.

The value of v1.4.2 lies in this mechanistic separation. Simpler versions are sufficient when the research question concerns demographic replacement or unconstrained population behaviour. The full formulation becomes useful when the question specifically concerns the coupling among demographic composition, subsistence demand, productive labour, and environmentally limited capacity.

11.8 Chapter conclusion

Version 1.4.2 provides the most complete demand-and-supply formulation in the present model series. Population pressure now represents the ratio between age-weighted demographic demand and the carrying capacity that the population can effectively realise through its age-weighted productive labour. Nominal environmental capacity sets the potential upper limit, while demographic composition determines both the demand placed on that limit and the labour available to realise it.

The resulting sensitivity hierarchy separates the functions of these mechanisms clearly. Labour demand per capita primarily governs pressure and viability; nominal environmental capacity sets the potential scale of effective capacity; density-effect strength determines the mortality consequence of pressure; and fertility and union formation remain the principal determinants of demographic composition.

Labour limitation does not impose a universal threshold. Populations can persist under partially realised carrying capacity, while favourable demographic configurations may maintain full productive capacity even at relatively high labour requirements. Whether labour scarcity produces regulation or extinction therefore depends on its interaction with the wider demographic configuration.

Version 1.4.2 consequently does not supersede the simpler formulations for every purpose. Rather, it provides the appropriate formulation when demographic demand, productive capacity, and their reciprocal feedback with age structure are themselves part of the research question.
