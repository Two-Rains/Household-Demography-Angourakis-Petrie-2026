A particularity in processing the data with age-weighted variables is that we need to use (or compute) the total carrying capacity demand (carrying_capacity_demand) and use it to calculate pressure instead of using totalIndividuals directly (i.e., pressure = carrying_capacity_demand / carrying_capacity_effective). Although this variable can be exported through NetLogo’s Behavior Space, it was not specified for all massive Sobol sampled experiments, so it is necessary to calculate it using womenAgeStructure and menAgeStructure (see “library/density/compute_carrying_capacity_demand.R” and “library/density/compute_effective_carrying_capacity.R”).

The introduction of labour-mediated effective carrying capacity produces the largest change in overall viability observed so far in the density-dependent model series. Only 8,659 of the 48,000 simulations, approximately 18.0%, avoid extinction, compared with about 62.5% in v1.2.1. The additional supply-side constraint therefore acts as a strong demographic viability filter: parameter configurations that remain viable when ecological pressure depends only on demand frequently collapse when the population must also generate sufficient labour to realise carrying capacity.

Residence rule and Coale-Demeny region remain secondary at the aggregate level. Matrilocal-matrilineal residence produces 4,361 surviving runs and patrilocal-patrilineal residence 4,298, a very small difference relative to the size of the experiment. Overshooting is similarly balanced between residence rules (539 and 541 cases, respectively). Thus, the substantial increase in extinction is attributable to the introduction of labour limitation across the parameter space rather than to a strong residence-specific shift in viability.

Household-level organisation remains broadly similar to v1.2.1. Population size scales closely with household number under both residence systems, and the sampled trajectories converge towards comparable ranges of mean household size. Among surviving populations, effective carrying capacity largely reproduces the distribution of nominal carrying capacity. The corresponding laboured_share values cluster very close to one, indicating that populations which remain viable generally generate sufficient labour to realise nearly the full environmental capacity available to them.

The labour-ratio distributions reinforce this pattern. Surviving populations commonly maintain available labour at or above the amount required, although considerable variation remains among individual runs and survival categories. Labour limitation therefore does not generally produce a continuum of strongly reduced equilibrium capacities among survivors.

Combined with the much higher extinction frequency in the global experiment, this points to a strong selection effect. Configurations unable to maintain adequate productive labour tend not to survive long enough to appear among the late-run distributions, whereas successful configurations have largely escaped direct labour limitation. Labour thus behaves primarily as a viability constraint in the yearly implementation: it determines whether the demographic system can sustain the environmental capacity available to it more strongly than it determines the equilibrium capacity of populations that have already survived.

This also explains why labour_demand_per_capita can become the strongest predictor of survival while remaining a comparatively weak predictor of effective carrying capacity conditional on survival. The two analyses describe different sides of the same filter.

All reference trajectories are classified as Overshooting under the current operational criteria, yet their late-run pressures have very low coefficients of variation. As in the previous density-dependent versions, overshooting in this targeted sample therefore represents stable regulation slightly above the relevant capacity threshold rather than uncontrolled population growth.

In v1.2.2, however, the ecological meaning of that threshold is more complex. Population pressure depends on age-weighted subsistence demand relative to the capacity that can actually be realised through available labour. Populations of similar abundance can therefore experience different pressure depending on both the demand generated by their age composition and the productive contribution of that composition.

The reference trajectories demonstrate that once labour sufficiency is maintained, stable high-pressure states remain possible. The principal effect of labour supply is therefore expressed more strongly in the global probability of reaching such a viable state than in the late-run variability of those trajectories that do.

Residence rule again has only a very small direct association with median age ((d=0.12)), while pressure regime remains much more informative. The important result is not that labour demand directly determines age structure, but that it changes which demographic configurations remain viable. Among those that survive, fertility and nuptiality continue to shape age composition more strongly than the labour parameter itself.

7.5.5 Interpretation

Version 1.2.2 produces a clear differentiation among the mechanisms controlling different model outcomes. labour_demand_per_capita becomes the dominant predictor of survival, density_effect_scaling_factor remains the strongest predictor of population pressure, fertility peak intensity continues to dominate median age, and nominal carrying_capacity overwhelmingly determines effective carrying capacity among survivors.

These distinct sensitivity hierarchies clarify the role of the newly introduced labour mechanism. Labour demand determines whether demographic configurations remain viable, but has relatively little direct influence on age structure or realised carrying capacity once populations have survived. Fertility and nuptiality continue to shape demographic composition, while density-dependent mortality regulates the pressure generated by that composition.

Labour supply therefore adds a new viability layer rather than replacing the demographic mechanisms identified in earlier versions. The model now distinguishes more explicitly between generating demographic demand, generating the labour required to meet that demand, and regulating the resulting population pressure.

Rather, it demonstrates that its effect is strongly conditional on demographic context. Across the full Sobol exploration, increasing labour requirements pushes many parameter combinations beyond their viability boundary; under this particular high-fertility, favourable-mortality configuration, however, the demographic system remains sufficiently productive throughout the tested range.

No meaningful residence-rule difference appears in this experiment. The previously inferred thresholds around 0.4–0.5 and 0.2–0.3 are therefore not supported by simulations and should be removed. 7.7 Discussion notes

Version 1.2.2 adds the supply side of demographic regulation by allowing age-dependent labour availability to determine how much of the nominal environmental carrying capacity can actually be realised. This produces a much stronger behavioural change than the demand weighting introduced in v1.2.1: the proportion of surviving simulations falls from approximately 62.5% to 18.0%.

The sensitivity analysis identifies the source of this shift clearly. labour_demand_per_capita becomes the strongest predictor of extinction, ahead of density-effect strength and fertility intensity. Labour availability therefore introduces a new demographic bottleneck: populations must not only generate sufficient reproductive replacement, but also maintain enough productive labour to sustain the carrying capacity on which that population depends.

At the same time, labour limitation is largely absent from the equilibrium states of populations that survive. Their laboured_share values cluster close to one and their effective carrying capacities consequently approach nominal carrying capacity. This apparent contrast is best understood as selection. Labour-deficient configurations disproportionately disappear, leaving a surviving subset that is predominantly labour-sufficient.

Population pressure remains governed most strongly by density_effect_scaling_factor, while fertility continues to determine much of the demographic demand placed on the system. Age structure likewise remains primarily controlled by fertility and nuptiality. The new labour mechanism therefore adds a viability constraint without replacing the demographic drivers already established in earlier versions.

The age structures of surviving populations nevertheless shift towards younger configurations relative to v1.2.1, and the contrast between low- and high-pressure populations becomes weaker. This is consistent with labour-dependent selection favouring populations containing sufficient productive-age cohorts, although the surviving-state comparisons alone cannot establish a unique causal pathway.

Residence rule continues to modify particular sex-specific nuptiality effects, but its aggregate effect on survival remains very small. Results therefore do not support a general claim that matrilocal-matrilineal populations are consistently more resilient across model versions.

Finally, the targeted labour-demand experiment demonstrates that the strong global influence of labour demand does not imply a universal deterministic threshold. Under the favourable reference configuration, both residence systems survive throughout the tested range. Labour demand instead alters viability in interaction with fertility, mortality, nuptiality, age structure, and environmental capacity.

7.8 Chapter conclusion

Version 1.2.2 completes the yearly demand-and-supply formulation by making realised carrying capacity contingent on the productive labour generated by the population itself. Its main effect is not to maintain surviving populations at persistently reduced carrying capacity, but to impose a strong viability filter: many demographic configurations collapse when they cannot generate sufficient labour, whereas those that survive generally realise nearly the full nominal environmental capacity.

This produces a clear division among model mechanisms. Labour demand primarily governs survival, density-effect strength governs population pressure, fertility and nuptiality govern demographic composition, and nominal carrying capacity governs the scale of effective capacity among surviving populations. Ecological regulation thus emerges from the interaction of external limits with age-dependent demographic demand and productive supply rather than from population abundance alone.

The model therefore establishes the full yearly demographic-ecological framework against which the subsequent daily-iteration versions can be evaluated.
